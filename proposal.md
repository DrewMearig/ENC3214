# The Effect of Logical Skill Development on Academic Performance
Drew Mearig - Department of Computer Science

Background

The development of logical reasoning skills is very closely tied to improved critical
thinking skills and academic performance. Activities that engage the logical centers of
your brain can develop a student’s ability to strategize, problem solve, and recognize
patterns. Research done by Sala and Gobet suggests that engaging in reasoning-based
activities like chess can improve the quality of performance in mathematics and
analytical subjects (2016). Additionally, research done by Adele Diamond has shown
that these same activities are linked to better memory, neuroplasticity and selfregulation. This project will examine the consistency of these effects on students’ testing
capabilities and academic performance.

Methodology

First, a benchmark assessment will be curated for determining a starting point for
participants. The assessment will include fifty short questions split into five parts:
inference logic, spatial reasoning, strategizing, sudoku, and reading comprehension.
Participants will be selected at random using a 10-dollar participation incentive to
motivate volunteers. Following selection, they will be given the first iteration of the
benchmark assessment. Once all scores have been recorded, the following five weeks
will be spent monitoring participants’ progress via self-report through a day-by-day
practice program. This program will include various forms of puzzle solving. Sudoku,
Chess, Logic Maps, Pattern Matching, and Spatial-based Lock Puzzles. Participants will
receive their objectives at the beginning of each day and report each night. At the end of
the five weeks, all participants will be retested on an equivalent assessment, and all
scores will be documented. Once testing is completed, all information will be compiled
and marked in any potential changes. Following that, there will be a conference
presentation in which all results will be covered thoroughly.

Anticipated Outcomes

This study is expected to show that those who engage in repeated logical training will
demonstrate heightened skills in academia. The expectations are as follows:

- Data charts showing valuable increase in assessment scores
- Information on what areas of logical process are most affected by training
- Conference presentation

Significance

This study aims to provide a better understanding of how the brain is affected by day-today training. Showing there is a positive impact on intellectual activity when active in
training the brain will give students, teachers, or anyone in an academic field a concrete
plan on how to maintain, improve, and evolve their brainpower. This is especially
important for teachers to help them understand how students learn and what helps them
to be more studious, not only in a single subject, but also learning the skills they need to
learn more efficiently.
